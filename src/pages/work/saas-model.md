## Why this exists

Most finance CVs list "financial modelling" as a skill. This is what I mean by it: a model with explicit drivers, honest assumptions, and an output a board could act on.

The company is real. The numbers are not: everything here is inferred from public sources (funding announcements, headcount, pricing pages, comparable benchmarks) and is marked as an assumption where I couldn't verify it.

## What's in the model

- ARR build by customer segment, with new, expansion, contraction and churned ARR shown as a waterfall
- Churn and net revenue retention assumptions, with the benchmark they're anchored to
- Customer acquisition cost by channel and gross-margin-adjusted payback in months
- Headcount plan and operating expenses
- Runway under a base, downside and upside case, with the month cash goes below six months of burn flagged
- A one-page board summary: the five numbers that matter and what changed since last month

## What I'd ask the founders in the first meeting

1. Which of my assumptions is furthest from reality, and in which direction?
2. What triggers a customer to churn, and how early do you see it?
3. What does the sales team need to believe about payback to justify the next hire?

## What I'd change with real data

Cohort-level retention instead of a blended rate, CAC split by inbound and outbound, and actual gross margin rather than a benchmark.
